The thesis

Every paid FPL tool optimises the wrong number

They all maximise expected points against the global field. That is the right objective for the fraction of a percent chasing a top-10k finish, and the wrong one for everyone else — because almost nobody's actual goal is a global rank. It's finishing above eight friends, a group chat, or an office league.

Those are different optimisation problems with different answers. If you're 40 points behind with six gameweeks left, the highest-expected-points transfer is the worst move available: copying the template locks in your deficit. What you need is variance, and specifically variance your rival isn't exposed to. No incumbent computes that, because none of them model your rivals.

The data to model them is free. The official FPL API exposes — with no authentication — every manager's squad for every gameweek, their transfers, their chip usage, and full mini-league standings. A live check on 31 August 2026 confirmed it open and serving.

The wedge

A different objective function, not a different interface

Every other FPL tool

  • Maximises expected points against 13 million strangers
  • Gives everyone the same answer, which is what makes mini-leagues unwinnable
  • Sells projections — a race we'd lose without an Opta licence
  • Has no idea who you're actually competing against
  • Free mini-league tools stop at descriptive statistics

Overtake

  • Maximises P(finish above a named person)
  • Prescribes variance when you're behind, and suppresses it when you're ahead
  • Treats projections as an input and publishes its own measured error
  • Reads every rival's real squad, transfers and chips from the public API
  • Simulates 20,000 seasons, then explains the result in one actionable sentence

Where AI sits. The LLM never computes a number. A seeded Monte Carlo engine produces the probabilities; the model turns that distribution into a named, specific argument, and every figure in its output is checked against the payload that produced it before display. A general chatbot fails this on data access and on computation — not on prompt quality. Bottom-up AI cost is $0.10–0.59 per paid user per month, roughly 3–16% of net revenue.

The money

€4.99 a month, or €29.99 for the season

The season pass is the default, and it solves three problems at once: it matches the natural unit of the product, it removes the June–July dead months from the churn calculation, and — as a one-time charge rather than a subscription — it avoids Stripe's recurring-billing fee entirely. It keeps 96.4% of face value against the monthly plan's 91.5%.

Fourteen days

The MVP exists to answer one question

Not "can this be built" — it plainly can. The question is whether a mini-league player will pay for rival-relative advice. Anything that doesn't help answer that is cut: no simulator, no chip planner, no chat, no Discord bot, no programmatic SEO. The FPL season is currently at gameweek 2, which is close to the ideal launch window — the alternative to starting now isn't a two-month delay, it's a year.

Days 1–3

Data foundation

Polite, cached ingest of every public FPL endpoint. By day 2 the database holds every rival's full squad for every completed gameweek. Projections are timeboxed to a single day — they're an input, not the product.

Days 4–5

The engine

Seeded Monte Carlo across the whole league, plus candidate-move deltas using common random numbers. The technical point of no return: if the engine's answer isn't believable here, stop before building any UI.

Days 6–8

The surface

Public league board, the rival dossier, share images, magic-link auth. Day 7's real test isn't a passing suite — it's whether two of three friends shown the dossier share it into a group chat unprompted.

Days 9–11

Money and narration

Prompt with grounding checks and a deterministic fallback, Stripe Checkout for both plans, entitlements enforced server-side, and the Deadline Brief email that creates the weekly return loop.

Days 12–13

Harden and beta

Analytics funnel, legal pages, rate limits, a hard in-code cap on daily AI spend, a tested backup restore. Then 30–50 real users through one full deadline cycle.

Day 14

Launch or stop

The r/FantasyPL post and the share loop — or a documented halt. The thresholds are written before launch precisely so that two weeks of sunk effort can't quietly rewrite them.

Go / no-go

Written down before launch, on purpose

Measured across the beta cohort over one gameweek cycle. GO requires the first, third and fifth to all clear — value delivered, habit formed, and money changing hands. Any one failing alone is a different diagnosis, not a smaller version of the same one.

North Star: Weekly Active Deciding Managers — unique users who open a rival dossier or Deadline Brief in the 72 hours before a deadline. Signups measure curiosity; DAU would reward notification spam for a product that should be used intensely twice a week; MRR arrives too late to steer by. WADM rising while MRR is flat means the pricing is wrong. WADM falling means the product is.

How it dies

The honest failure analysis

CriticalNobody pays. The most likely failure. FPL managers may enjoy arguing about the decision more than being handed it — and every free mini-league analyser on the market being free is evidence worth taking seriously rather than explaining away.
CriticalAcquisition stalls at 200. With no budget, a flat Reddit launch and a near-zero share loop leave no affordable plan B. Measure k-factor from week one; SEO is the fallback, with an honest six-to-nine-month lag.
SeriousThe FPL API is restricted. Public, undocumented, unlicensed — a decade-old ecosystem exists on it, but nothing is promised. Mitigated by caching, politeness, a degradation mode and a manual-import fallback behind an interface.
SeriousSeasonality. Ten weeks a year with no product, in a business too young to have reserves. Season-pass pricing absorbs it; Champions League Fantasy in V2 fills the calendar.
SeriousAn incumbent ships a rival module. Fantasy Football Scout already bundles a mini-league product. Speed and focus buy time here, not safety.

On the moat: there isn't one on day one. There's a wedge and roughly a six-to-twelve-month head start. The only asset that genuinely compounds is accumulated league memory — a behavioural history for every rival in every tracked league, which a follower arriving next season cannot retroactively acquire. Anyone claiming a defensible moat at launch is selling something.

What would make me abandon this. Fewer than ten volunteered league IDs in the two-day concierge test. Aha rate and return rate both under 25% in beta. Zero paid conversions after two distinct pricing attempts. The Premier League restricting API access with no workable fallback. Or any incumbent shipping a genuine rival-relative simulator first — being second with the same idea and 1% of the distribution is not a business.

And the evidence that it's working arrives in August 2027: season-over-season renewal above 50%. Everything before that is a promising start.
